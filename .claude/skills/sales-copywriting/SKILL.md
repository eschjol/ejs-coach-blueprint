# Sales Copywriting — Cure Your Slice Funnel

Purpose: produce paste-ready page copy for every step of the funnel. Facts,
prices, and proof points come from `ghl/cure-your-slice/offer.md` — read it
before writing. Save finished copy to `ghl/cure-your-slice/copy/` (one file per
page) so it can be pasted into GHL and versioned.

## Erik's voice

Coach-to-student: confident, plain, specific. Data over mysticism (TrackMan 4,
3D video, pressure plates). Signature idea: **get better from day one — no
"get worse to get better."** No hype-bro tone, no fake countdown timers, no
"gurus hate him." A $27 product earns trust for a $795/mo relationship; write
like the coach they'll eventually pay $795/month.

## What each page must do

### Sales page (long-form, single CTA: buy $27)

Structure to follow — every section earns the scroll to the next:

1. **Hook headline** — name the enemy (the slice) + the promise mechanism.
   Pattern that works: problem + timeframe-free fix. "Your Slice Isn't a
   Talent Problem. It's a Face-to-Path Problem — and It's Fixable."
2. **Agitate with recognition, not shame** — the ball starting left and
   carving right, aiming further left every round, the 3-fairways-over drive.
   The reader should think "he's watched me play."
3. **The mechanism** — why slices happen (open face relative to path,
   out-to-in swing) in plain words. Teaching a real thing builds the trust
   that sells; a page that could have been written by a marketer converts
   worse than one that could only be written by a coach.
4. **The product** — what's inside the video series, watched in an afternoon,
   drills included. Frame $27 as less than a sleeve-of-balls-per-round habit
   of slicing.
5. **Proof** — Erik's credentials, student results/testimonials from
   ejsgolf.com, tech stack. Real quotes only; never fabricate testimonials —
   leave clearly marked placeholders if none are on hand.
6. **Offer recap + guarantee** (30-day money-back unless Erik says otherwise)
7. **FAQ** — is this for my swing, how long, equipment needed, refunds.
8. **Final CTA.** Repeat the buy button every 1.5–2 scroll-screens.

### Order bump box (on checkout)

The highest-leverage 40 words in the funnel. Formula: checkbox headline states
the upgrade and price delta plainly, 2–3 lines connect it to what they just
decided to fix. Example shape:

> **☑ YES — upgrade my order to $79 and add the Ball Striking Machine
> Blueprint.** Fixing your slice gets the ball in play. The Blueprint is the
> next step: the full system for compressing irons and striking it pure —
> normally sold separately, added to your library instantly.

One bump only. No second checkbox, no downsell clutter on the checkout.

### Upsell 1 — Swing Analysis $279

Congratulate → gap → offer. "The videos fix the pattern; this makes it
personal": Erik personally analyzes their swing video, frame by frame, and
sends a prioritized fix plan. Emphasize one-click ("your card won't be charged
until you click"). Decline link must be visible but quiet ("No thanks, take me
to my videos").

### Upsell 2 — Online Program $795/mo

Only serious buyers see this page; write it straight. Ongoing coaching with
Erik: monthly structure, swing reviews, direct access, the same system his
in-person students pay more for. Anchor against in-person lesson pricing
($150/hr) and state clearly it's monthly, cancel anytime (confirm terms with
Erik before publishing).

### Thank-you page

Access instructions (check email, member login link), what to do first (watch
lesson 1 today), and one soft ascension line for whichever upsell they skipped.

## Compliance guardrails

No earnings-style guarantees or "add X yards guaranteed." Fixes: promise the
mechanism and the process. This keeps the page usable as a Google Ads landing
page (see `google-ads-search` for landing-page policy notes).

## Output format

One markdown file per page with GHL-ready sections (headline / subhead / body /
CTA blocks), placeholders in {{curly braces}} only for genuinely variable
values. After drafting, do a second pass cutting 20% of the words — first
drafts of sales copy always run long.
